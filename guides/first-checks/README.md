# Vexon Free First-Check Helper

A few gentle checks for common home and small-office problems. This free guide helps narrow what to ask next; it does not diagnose or guarantee a repair.

Use only equipment you own or are authorized to use. Save work before restarting. Follow your organization’s support process for managed or shared equipment.

This guide has no form fields or upload requests. Its section links work without JavaScript and do not send answers to Vexon. Your browser or hosting service may retain ordinary visit records. Do not send passwords, codes or private account details.

## Choose the issue

- [Internet or Wi-Fi](#wifi-start)
- [Printer trouble](#printer-start)

Follow only the section your choice points to. You can stop and ask the equipment owner or support provider at any point.

## Internet or Wi-Fi

<a id="wifi-start"></a>

### Is it one device or the network?

Try two familiar websites on the affected device. If available, compare another device on the same network; a phone using mobile data is not the same test.

Do this only on a network and equipment you are allowed to use.

- [Only one device has trouble](#wifi-device)
- [Several devices have trouble](#wifi-shared)
- [I cannot compare](#wifi-handoff)

References: [Microsoft: Fix Wi-Fi connection issues in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/connectivity-networking/fix-wi-fi-connection-issues-in-windows).

<a id="wifi-device"></a>

### Check this device first

Confirm it is connected to the Wi-Fi network you recognize. Move nearer the router if practical.

Save your work. With the owner’s permission, restart this device normally once and retry the same two websites.

- [It works now](#all-working)
- [Still stuck or unsure](#wifi-handoff)

References: [Microsoft: Fix Wi-Fi connection issues in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/connectivity-networking/fix-wi-fi-connection-issues-in-windows).

<a id="wifi-shared"></a>

### Check for a shared interruption

Look at the router’s power and status indicators without unplugging or changing shared equipment.

Use a separate working connection, if available, to check your internet provider’s official outage page. A working Wi-Fi signal alone does not prove internet access.

- [The provider reports an outage](#wifi-provider)
- [No clear outage, or I cannot check](#wifi-handoff)

References: [Microsoft: Fix Wi-Fi connection issues in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/connectivity-networking/fix-wi-fi-connection-issues-in-windows), [Apple: If your Mac is not connecting over Wi-Fi](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101588).

<a id="wifi-provider"></a>

### Follow the provider’s update

Use the provider’s official instructions and status updates. Avoid repeatedly changing devices while a known outage continues.

If this is a business network, tell its administrator what still works.

References: [Microsoft: Fix Wi-Fi connection issues in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/connectivity-networking/fix-wi-fi-connection-issues-in-windows), [Apple: If your Mac is not connecting over Wi-Fi](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101588).

<a id="wifi-handoff"></a>

### Stop and hand off the facts

Tell the equipment owner, administrator or provider whether one or several devices fail, the exact message, and which familiar sites still work.

Do not reset the router, remove drivers or change security/network settings through this helper.

References: [Microsoft: Fix Wi-Fi connection issues in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/connectivity-networking/fix-wi-fi-connection-issues-in-windows).

## Printer trouble

<a id="printer-start"></a>

### Does the printer show a problem?

Check power, paper, visible ink/toner warnings and the printer’s own display. Look for a loose cable without disturbing other equipment.

For a network printer, confirm you are using the expected local network. A working internet connection alone does not show that the printer is reachable.

- [There is an error or warning](#printer-warning)
- [It looks ready](#printer-queue)
- [It is shared or I am unsure](#printer-handoff)

References: [Microsoft: Fix printer connection and printing problems in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/hardware/printer/fix-printer-connection-and-printing-problems-in-windows).

<a id="printer-warning"></a>

### Use the printer’s exact message

Follow the model’s official instructions for the displayed paper, ink or other warning only if you own or are allowed to maintain it.

Do not force stuck parts or clear a shared printer’s jobs. If the error remains, pass its exact wording to the owner or vendor.

References: [Microsoft: Fix printer connection and printing problems in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/hardware/printer/fix-printer-connection-and-printing-problems-in-windows).

<a id="printer-queue"></a>

### Check the destination and queue

Look at the selected printer name and its queue. A paused, offline or held job is useful information; do not delete other people’s jobs.

If the owner permits and no shared queue would be disrupted, try one small test page. Repeatedly clicking Print can create duplicates.

- [The page prints](#all-working)
- [It does not print or I am unsure](#printer-handoff)

References: [Microsoft: Fix printer connection and printing problems in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/hardware/printer/fix-printer-connection-and-printing-problems-in-windows).

<a id="printer-handoff"></a>

### Keep the printer record simple

Give the owner or vendor the printer model, exact display/queue message, cable or network connection, and whether any other device can print.

Stop before reinstalling drivers, clearing a shared queue or factory resetting the printer.

References: [Microsoft: Fix printer connection and printing problems in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/hardware/printer/fix-printer-connection-and-printing-problems-in-windows).

## Slow app or device

<a id="slow-start"></a>

### Is one app slow, or most things?

Notice whether only one app struggles or the whole device is slow. If it is only a website, compare another familiar site.

Save any work you can before closing an app or restarting.

- [One app or site](#slow-app)
- [Most things on the device](#slow-device)
- [Work is unsaved or this is managed equipment](#slow-handoff)

References: [Apple: Quit apps on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/quit-apps-mchl834d18c2/mac), [Microsoft: Restart your PC](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/restart-reboot-your-pc-110262aa-fc79-1c33-7b00-c140ae3a6dac).

<a id="slow-app"></a>

### Try one normal app restart

Close the app using its normal Quit or Exit option after saving work, then reopen it once. Closing a window does not always quit the app.

If one online service alone is affected, check that service’s official status/help page.

- [It responds normally now](#all-working)
- [Still slow or cannot close safely](#slow-handoff)

References: [Apple: Quit apps on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/quit-apps-mchl834d18c2/mac).

<a id="slow-device"></a>

### Try a normal device restart

With the owner’s permission, save work and restart the device using its normal menu. Wait for it to finish, then retry the same task.

Do not force the power off, delete files or install a cleaner through this helper.

- [It responds normally now](#all-working)
- [Still slow or restart is not suitable](#slow-handoff)

References: [Microsoft: Restart your PC](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/restart-reboot-your-pc-110262aa-fc79-1c33-7b00-c140ae3a6dac).

<a id="slow-handoff"></a>

### Stop before risking work

Tell the owner or support provider which apps are affected, when it began, the exact message and whether a normal restart was tried.

If work cannot be saved or the device belongs to an employer, ask its administrator before forcing an app closed or changing settings.

References: [Apple: Quit apps on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/quit-apps-mchl834d18c2/mac), [Microsoft: Restart your PC](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/restart-reboot-your-pc-110262aa-fc79-1c33-7b00-c140ae3a6dac).

## Unexpected or unsafe-looking link

<a id="link-start"></a>

### What happened with the unexpected link?

Do not enter a password, payment information or verification code. A secure connection or HTTPS does not prove a site is honest.

Do not follow a page’s request to install software, run a command or disable protection.

- [I have not opened it](#link-unopened)
- [I opened it but entered nothing and ran nothing](#link-opened)
- [I entered information, opened a download or ran something](#link-escalate)
- [I am unsure](#link-escalate)

References: [FTC: How to recognize and avoid phishing scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-avoid-phishing-scams), [FTC: Online shopping](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping).

<a id="link-unopened"></a>

### Use a route you already trust

Leave the link unopened. If the message might matter, open the organization’s known website yourself or contact it using a verified number.

For a work message, use the organization’s phishing-report process.

References: [FTC: How to recognize and avoid phishing scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-avoid-phishing-scams).

<a id="link-opened"></a>

### Leave the page and do not continue

Close the unexpected page. Do not open an unexpected download or return to the page to investigate.

If you notice a download, warning or unusual behavior, use official security support; managed devices go to their administrator. Opening a page alone does not establish whether the device is safe or compromised.

- [No other action; I want the trusted route](#link-unopened)
- [A warning, download or unusual behavior appeared](#link-escalate)

References: [FTC: How to recognize and avoid phishing scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-avoid-phishing-scams).

<a id="link-escalate"></a>

### Use official account or security help

Stop interacting with the page and contact your administrator if this is a work device.

From a device you trust, open the real account provider’s security/help page for any information you entered. Follow its recovery instructions. For a file or program you ran, follow your device vendor’s security guidance. Do not send secrets to this helper or to an unverified “recovery” contact.

References: [FTC: How to recognize and avoid phishing scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-avoid-phishing-scams).

## If the retry works

<a id="all-working"></a>

### It works for now

Stop making changes. Remember what you tried and what changed in case the problem returns.

A successful retry is useful evidence; it does not prove the underlying cause.
